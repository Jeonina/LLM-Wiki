**Citation:** McKenna et al. (2016) — *Whole-organism lineage tracing by combinatorial and cumulative genome editing (GESTALT)* — *Science*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf7907)

McKenna, Findlay, Gagnon, Horwitz, Schier and Shendure introduced GESTALT (genome editing of synthetic target arrays for lineage tracing), the founding CRISPR-Cas9 combinatorial-barcode lineage-tracing method. A synthetic array of 9–12 CRISPR/Cas9 target sites (10-target version is 257 bp) is integrated into the host genome; during development, Cas9 and a cocktail of guide RNAs progressively introduce diverse, irreversible insertion/deletion edits at the target sites. Each cell accumulates a unique edited barcode that records its developmental history, readable by single-amplicon sequencing of either DNA or RNA from each cell.

In cell-culture proof-of-principle, GESTALT generated $\sim$1,650 uniquely edited barcodes from a single transfected population. Applied to zebrafish by zygote injection, GESTALT recorded thousands of distinct barcodes per animal and from $\sim$200,000 cells per individual was able to infer that most cells in each adult organ derive from a small number of progenitor cells, and that ancestral progenitors contribute nonuniformly to germ layers and organ systems.

## Why this matters

The founding combinatorial-genome-editing lineage-tracing method, parent to a family of approaches (scGESTALT, ScarTrace, LINNAEUS, MARC1, CARLIN). Synthetic-barcode lineage tracing complements natural-mutation lineage tracing (Lodato 2015, Coorens 2021, Lee-Six 2018) and methylation-epimutation lineage tracing (EPI-Clone) by occupying the high-resolution, transgenic-system axis.
