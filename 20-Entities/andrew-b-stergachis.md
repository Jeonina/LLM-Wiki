# Andrew B. Stergachis

> Genome scientist and clinical geneticist at University of Washington; senior author of the DAF-seq paper (2025) and prior developer of Fiber-seq. Group focuses on single-molecule, single-nucleotide chromatin architecture mapping.

## Profile

Department of Genome Sciences and Division of Medical Genetics, University of Washington School of Medicine, Seattle; affiliated with the Brotman Baty Institute for Precision Medicine. Email absterga@uw.edu. The [[30-Concepts/fiber-seq]] → [[30-Concepts/daf-seq]] arc is from this group: Fiber-seq established m6A methyltransferase stenciling for bulk single-molecule chromatin readouts; DAF-seq replaces the methylation marks with deaminations that survive amplification, enabling single-cell extension.

## Mentions

- **2026-05-07** — [[10-Summaries/swanson-2025-daf-seq]]: senior/corresponding author. Established DAF-seq and scDAF-seq.

## Positions and claims

- Holds that **single-molecule chromatin assays must capture protein occupancy at near-nucleotide resolution** to disentangle TF cooperativity, haplotype-specific regulation, and chromatin actuation states — readouts that ensemble assays smear out ([[10-Summaries/swanson-2025-daf-seq]]).
- Holds that **chromosome-length single-cell chromatin profiling** is achievable by combining cytidine deamination footprinting with primary template-directed amplification, yielding ~99% genome coverage per cell ([[10-Summaries/swanson-2025-daf-seq]]).
