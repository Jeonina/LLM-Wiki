# Margaret A. Goodell

> Hematopoietic stem cell biologist; director of the Center for Cell and Gene Therapy at Baylor College of Medicine. Long research line on HSC quiescence, DNMT3A function, and the link between hematopoietic stem cell aging and clonal hematopoiesis. Senior/corresponding author on the [[10-Summaries/kapadia-2024-stem-cell-aging|Kapadia & Goodell 2024]] Nature Aging review.

## Profile

Affiliations: Department of Molecular and Cellular Biology, Center for Cell and Gene Therapy, Baylor College of Medicine, Houston TX. Email goodell@bcm.edu. The Goodell lab has been central to defining the molecular features of HSC quiescence and the consequences of *DNMT3A* loss — one of the top two clonal-hematopoiesis drivers.

## Mentions

- **2026-05-14** — [[10-Summaries/kapadia-2024-stem-cell-aging]]: senior/corresponding author. Frames the lockstep between stem cell aging and somatic mosaicism, using HSC as the quantitatively tractable model and operationalizing each "hallmark of aging" for stem cells.

## Positions and claims

- Stem cell aging and somatic mosaicism progress in lockstep; mosaicism is the substrate on which aged tissue evolution acts ([[10-Summaries/kapadia-2024-stem-cell-aging]]).
- Cell-intrinsic aging changes (genomic instability, epigenetic drift, metabolic shift, proteostasis loss) create the selective environment that favors specific clonal outgrowths — "adaptive oncogenesis" ([[10-Summaries/kapadia-2024-stem-cell-aging]]).
- Whole-genome duplex-consensus sequencing will be critical for new driver discovery and characterization of early clonal interplay ([[10-Summaries/kapadia-2024-stem-cell-aging]]).
