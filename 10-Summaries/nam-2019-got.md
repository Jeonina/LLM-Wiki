**Citation:** Nam et al. (2019) — *Genotyping of Transcriptomes (GoT) links somatic mutations to cell identity* — *Nature*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1367-0)

Nam, Kim, Chaligne et al. (Landau, Smibert labs; NYGC + Weill Cornell + MSKCC) developed **Genotyping of Transcriptomes (GoT)**, a method integrating targeted genotyping with droplet-based scRNA-seq. Modification to the 10x Genomics workflow: a locus-specific PCR amplifies the gene-of-interest from cDNA before transcript-end sequencing; genotype is linked to the transcriptome via the shared cell barcode. Applied to 38,290 CD34+ cells from patients with CALR-mutated myeloproliferative neoplasms (essential thrombocythemia), GoT revealed an increasing fitness advantage with myeloid differentiation, the unfolded-protein response as the dominant transcriptional outcome of CALR mutation, and NF-κB pathway upregulation specifically in uncommitted stem cells. The toolkit also supports genotyping multiple targets and loci distant from transcript ends.

## Why this matters

Founding paper for the **GoT family** — a key §3.1/§4 reference for genotyping-aware single-cell omics. Predecessor of GoT-ChA (Izzo 2024, chromatin accessibility), GoT-IM (immune repertoire), and the Tapestri-DAb / scWGS-RNA combinations. Crucial when discussing CH/MPN applications (§5) — CALR/JAK2 mutations in HSCs are canonical CH drivers and GoT provides the cell-type-specific transcriptional consequence of those mutations. From Dan Landau's lab — major figure in CH and clonal-evolution single-cell methodology.
