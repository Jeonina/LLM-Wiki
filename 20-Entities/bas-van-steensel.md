# Bas van Steensel

> Group leader at the Netherlands Cancer Institute (NKI), Amsterdam; pioneer of DamID and the LAD field. His lab developed the DamID method (Pickersgill 2006), mapped the first genome-wide LAD landscape (Guelen 2008), and developed single-cell DamID with Jop Kind (Kind 2013, 2015).

## Profile

- Affiliation: Division of Gene Regulation, NKI (Netherlands Cancer Institute)
- Research focus: nuclear architecture, lamina-associated domains, DNA-protein interactions, chromatin organization
- Methodological signature: DamID and its single-cell variants

## Mentions

- **2026-05-19** — [[10-Summaries/van-steensel-2017-lads-review]]: senior co-author (with Andrew Belmont) of the canonical LADs review in *Cell* (2017). Articulates the cLAD/fLAD distinction, the multivalent/redundant anchoring model, and the three-compartment competition framework (NL/nucleoli/pericentromeric).

## Positions and claims

- LADs are organized via redundant H3K9 methyltransferases (G9a for me2; SUV39H1/2 for me3) — no single enzyme is necessary ([[10-Summaries/van-steensel-2017-lads-review]]).
- cLADs form a structural "backbone" anchoring chromosomes to the NL at conserved genomic positions, even though their DNA sequences are not conserved between species ([[10-Summaries/van-steensel-2017-lads-review]]).
- NL contact alone is probably *not* sufficient for gene repression — the heterochromatin compartment itself (not the NL surface per se) likely does the silencing work ([[10-Summaries/van-steensel-2017-lads-review]]).
