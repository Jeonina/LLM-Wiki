# O'Roak et al. 2012 — autism MIP-based resequencing

> Thesis: prior exome studies of autism (ASD) found many *de novo* mutations but few recurrently disrupted genes. O'Roak et al. modified **molecular inversion probes (MIPs)** to enable cost-effective targeted resequencing of 44 candidate ASD genes across thousands of patients — pinpointing genes that together contribute to **~1% of sporadic ASD**, supporting the multi-gene polygenic etiology.

## Key claims (abstract + intro)

- **Method**: modified molecular-inversion-probe (MIP) capture allows targeted resequencing of dozens of candidate genes from large cohorts cheaply.
- **Applied to 44 candidate genes** in thousands of probands and controls.
- **Several de novo recurrent mutations identified** in genes that collectively explain ~1% of sporadic ASD.
- Supports **highly polygenic architecture** of ASD — many low-frequency contributors rather than few high-frequency drivers.

## Why this matters

Pioneering example of **cost-effective targeted resequencing** for genetic-architecture studies of complex disease. Although bulk-tissue rather than single-cell, it establishes the autism genetics baseline against which later single-cell brain mosaicism studies (Lodato 2017, Bae 2018, McConnell 2013, Bizzotto 2022) are interpreted. Anchors the autism axis of the brain-mosaicism topic.
