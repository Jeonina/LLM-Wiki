# Epigenetic memory

> The mitotically heritable record of past cellular states encoded in DNA methylation, histone modifications, and chromatin organization. Cells "remember" their developmental history, tissue of origin, and exposure history even after dedifferentiation or reprogramming.

## Definition

Memory is enforced by **maintenance machinery**: DNMT1+UHRF1 at the replication fork, polycomb (H3K27me3) and trithorax (H3K4me3) systems, heterochromatin self-templating. Stable but not absolute: extended reprogramming or cancer-associated remodeling can erase memory partially.

## Why it matters

- **iPSCs retain methylation signatures** of donor cells and preferentially differentiate back toward original lineage ([[10-Summaries/kim-2017-methylation-memory-review]]).
- **Cancer cells retain methylation** of their tissue of origin even at metastatic sites — enables [[30-Concepts/cancer-of-unknown-primary]] tissue classification by methylation pattern (EPICUP).
- **MSCs** show methylation patterns that reflect their cell type of origin and remain stable through long-term culture, a quality-control marker ([[10-Summaries/kim-2017-methylation-memory-review]]).

## Added 2026-10-07

With CRISPR lineage barcodes as ground truth, clonally related mouse HSCs were significantly more similar in genome-wide CpG methylation (p = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰; 19 of 21 clones individually significant) but barely or not at all in gene expression (p = 0.049) or GpC accessibility (p = 0.34) [[10-Summaries/li-2023-darlin]]. The 279 clone-differential CpG regions were not near differentially regulated genes or GO-enriched, which the authors read as random loci [[10-Summaries/li-2023-darlin]]. This complements the earlier finding that sister cells share fate bias not visible in scRNA-seq [[10-Summaries/weinreb-2020-larry]].
