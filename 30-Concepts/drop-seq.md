# Drop-seq

> Droplet-microfluidic scRNA-seq method that co-encapsulates single cells with barcoded oligo-dT-coated microparticles in nanoliter aqueous droplets, capturing each cell's mRNA on its companion bead for parallel high-throughput transcriptome profiling.

## Definition

Drop-seq generates **STAMPs** (Single-cell Transcriptomes Attached to Microparticles) by: (1) co-flowing a cell suspension and a bead suspension in a microfluidic device across an oil channel to form ~1-nL droplets at ~100,000/min; (2) lysing cells inside droplets; (3) capturing poly-A mRNAs on the bead's oligo-dT primers, which also carry a constant adapter, a unique-per-bead cell barcode (12 nt, ~17M combinations from split-pool synthesis), and a per-primer UMI (8 nt random); (4) breaking droplets, pooling STAMPs, performing bulk reverse transcription and 3'-end library prep. The cell-of-origin is recovered post-hoc from the cell barcode embedded in each read. See [[10-Summaries/macosko-2015-drop-seq|Macosko 2015]] for the founding paper.

## Key performance numbers

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Cells per 12-h run | ~10,000 |
| Cost per cell | ~$0.065 |
| Transcript capture rate | ~12.8% (UMI-collapsed); ddPCR-confirmed at 10.7% |
| Cell-barcode space | 4¹² ≈ 17M combinations |
| Doublet rate | 0.36% (at 12.5 cells/µL) → 11.3% (at 100 cells/µL) |
| Single-cell purity | 98.8% → 90.4% across the same range |

## Why it matters

Drop-seq + inDrop (Klein et al., same Cell issue) **made the cell-atlas era possible**. Prior scRNA-seq protocols topped out at low thousands of cells per study at $10+ per cell; Drop-seq made tens of thousands routine at $0.07 per cell, directly enabling the Human Cell Atlas, Tabula Muris/Sapiens, and per-disease atlases. The commercial 10x Genomics Chromium platform is a refinement of the Drop-seq architecture (gel beads instead of solid beads, dissolvable on activation), and most droplet-based multi-omics methods (10x Multiome, CITE-seq, GoT, DOGMA-seq, Tapestri DAb-seq) inherit Drop-seq's bead + droplet + cell-barcode design.

## Bead architecture

Each Drop-seq bead carries millions of copies of an oligo with four concatenated parts (5' → 3'):

```
[PCR/sequencing primer site] [cell barcode 12nt] [UMI 8nt] [oligo-dT capture (T30)]
```

- The cell barcode is built by **split-and-pool synthesis**: pool of beads is split into 4 wells (A/G/C/T), one base added per well, recombined, re-split — repeated 12 times — yielding 4¹² = 16,777,216 distinct cell barcodes, identical across all primers on a single bead but different between beads.
- The UMI is generated by 8 rounds of degenerate synthesis, giving 4⁸ = 65,536 per-molecule tags per bead — enough to distinguish PCR duplicates from genuine reuse.
- The oligo-dT captures poly-A mRNAs.

This bead design is the conceptual basis for 10x gel beads, sciRNA-seq combinatorial barcodes, and the bead-barcode component of nearly every droplet-based multi-omics method.

## Limitations

- **3'-end only**: no isoform resolution. Full-length protocols (Smart-seq2/3) trade throughput for transcript coverage.
- **Ambient RNA contamination**: cell-suspension preparation releases mRNA from damaged cells; this background is captured non-specifically and inflates rare-cell expression of high-abundance markers (e.g. rod-cell genes appearing in non-rod retinal clusters).
- **Gene dropout**: ~13% capture rate → systematic zero-inflation for low-expressed genes. See [[10-Summaries/svensson-2017-power-analysis|Svensson 2017]] for quantitative benchmark.
- **Doublet–concentration tradeoff**: pushing throughput increases doublet rate (Poisson loading); doublet detection (Scrublet, DoubletFinder) is now standard post-processing.
