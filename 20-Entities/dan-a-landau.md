# Dan A. Landau

> Hematologist–oncologist and computational biologist; senior/corresponding author on both the GoT (2019) and GoT–ChA (2024) papers. PI of the [[20-Entities/landau-lab|Landau Lab]] at the New York Genome Center / Weill Cornell.

## Profile

Affiliations: New York Genome Center, Division of Hematology and Medical Oncology at Weill Cornell Medicine, Sandra and Edward Meyer Cancer Center, Institute for Computational Biomedicine. Email dlandau@nygenome.org. Long-running research line on clonal evolution and subclonal architecture of hematological malignancies (CLL, MPN). The methods program in the lab has produced [[30-Concepts/got]] → [[30-Concepts/got-cha]] over five years.

## Mentions

- **2026-05-07** — [[10-Summaries/nam-2019-got]]: senior/corresponding author. Established GoT and the cell-identity-dependent framing of CALR mutation effects.
- **2026-05-07** — [[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]]: senior/corresponding author. Extended the methods program to single-cell chromatin via GoT–ChA and identified cell-intrinsic pro-inflammatory chromatin priming in JAK2V617F-mutant HSCs.

## Positions and claims

- Holds that **somatic mutation effects must be measured in their native cell-identity context** — the same mutation produces different outputs in different progenitor states, and this is invisible to bulk or uncoupled single-cell assays ([[10-Summaries/nam-2019-got]], [[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]]).
- Holds that **co-capture of genotype with phenotype (RNA, chromatin, protein) in the same cell** is the right unit of analysis for studying clonal evolution in human samples ([[10-Summaries/nam-2019-got]], [[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]]).
- Holds that **inflammation in MPN has a cell-intrinsic chromatin component** — not solely microenvironmental — visible at the JAK2V617F clonal hematopoiesis stage ([[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]]).
