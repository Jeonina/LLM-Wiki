# DNase-seq

> Original genome-scale chromatin accessibility assay using DNase I endonuclease cleavage of accessible (nucleosome-free) DNA; it established the chromatin-accessibility paradigm as ENCODE's original assay, but has been largely superseded by [[atac-seq]] because it needs millions of cells ([[10-Summaries/klemm-2019-chromatin-accessibility-review|Klemm 2019]]).

> [!abstract] 한눈에
> DNase-seq는 DNase I이 nucleosome이 없는 열린 DNA를 자르는 성질로 chromatin accessibility를 처음 유전체 규모로 측정한 ENCODE의 원조 assay다 ([[10-Summaries/klemm-2019-chromatin-accessibility-review|Klemm 2019]]). 수백만 개의 세포가 필요해서 약 500개 세포로 되는 ATAC-seq에 대부분 자리를 내주었다 ([[10-Summaries/klemm-2019-chromatin-accessibility-review|Klemm 2019]]).

## Definition

DNase I preferentially cleaves DNA at nucleosome-free, TF-accessible regions, the DNase I hypersensitive sites (DHSs) ([[10-Summaries/klemm-2019-chromatin-accessibility-review|Klemm 2019]]). Two original protocols (synthesis):

- **Boyle et al. 2008 (single cut)** — restriction-enzyme-mediated isolation and barcoding of each DNase cut site.
- **Hesselberth et al. 2009 (double cut)** — size selection for fragments arising from paired cleavage events within DHSs.

## Why it matters

- Defined the field of [[chromatin-accessibility|chromatin accessibility]] prior to ATAC-seq ([[10-Summaries/klemm-2019-chromatin-accessibility-review|Klemm 2019]]).
- The ENCODE DHS atlas (>100 tissues, cell lines) was generated by DNase-seq and remains a benchmark for accessibility comparisons.
- Sequence-bias profile differs from Tn5 (ATAC-seq) and can be useful for footprinting in some contexts.

## Relation to ATAC-seq

- Largely supplanted by [[atac-seq]] for new experiments due to the input-requirement gap: millions of cells versus ~500 ([[10-Summaries/klemm-2019-chromatin-accessibility-review|Klemm 2019]]).

## Evidence and examples

- ENCODE Tier 1 cell line DHS profiles established the observation that ~2–3% of the genome is accessible yet contains >90% of TF binding, referenced throughout [[10-Summaries/klemm-2019-chromatin-accessibility-review|Klemm 2019]].

## Contested points

- DNase-seq vs ATAC-seq peak calls show small but real differences at fine scale — most consequential for footprinting.
