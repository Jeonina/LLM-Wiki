# Alzheimer's disease

> A progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by amyloid-β plaques, neurofibrillary tau tangles, and cognitive decline. Strong genetic risk from rare familial mutations (APP, PSEN1/2) and common variants (APOE, CLU, PICALM, BIN1), but most sporadic cases lack a clear single genetic cause.

## Definition

AD is the most common form of dementia. ~5% of cases are early-onset familial; the rest are late-onset and multifactorial.

## Why it matters

- Inherited variants explain only a fraction of AD risk. Cell-type-specific somatic mosaicism is an emerging hypothesis for additional risk: [[10-Summaries/kousi-2022-ad-mosaicism]] (Kousi et al. 2022) reports cell-type-specific somatic mutational burden in Alzheimer's dementia (preprint; only its reference list is clipped, so AD-vs-control specifics are unverified).
- Cumulative somatic SNV burden in neurons increases with age and correlates with neurodegeneration (Lodato et al. 2018; reviewed in [[10-Summaries/bizzotto-2022-brain-mosaicism-review]]).
- ROSMAP cohort provides multi-omic AD brain data (RNA, DNA, ATAC, methylation) at single-cell resolution.

## Added 2026-10-07

AD SNP heritability is most strongly enriched in microglia-specific enhancer–promoter target regions called by SnapHiC-G from sn-m3C-seq (S-LDSC P = 1.07 × 10⁻⁶), and an AD variant (rs1880949) is linked to the microglia-expressed gene ARPC1B ([[10-Summaries/liu-2024-snaphic-g]]).
