# Forsberg 2017 — the structural-variant-centric framing of somatic mosaicism

> Influential 2017 NRG review on post-zygotic genetic variation in normal human soma. Unlike the SNV-centric CHIP framing dominant in the 2018+ literature, Forsberg et al. emphasize that **structural variants (CNVs, LOY, CNNLOH) leave a 50–100× larger genomic footprint than point mutations** and are the *most common* category of aberrant clonal expansion (ACE) in aging. Introduces the **ACE** terminology, documents **loss of chromosome Y (LOY)** as the most common human post-zygotic mutation (affecting ~20% of men ≥70), and frames Turner syndrome's frequent mosaicism as the prototype showing that mosaic-rescue lineages may be necessary for live birth in otherwise embryonic-lethal aneuploidies.

## Key claims

1. **No two cells in an adult human soma share an identical genome.** Lynch's 2010 estimate of >10¹⁶ point mutations across the adult body, plus a 50–100× larger footprint from indels/SVs, makes per-cell genetic identity vanishingly unlikely.
2. **ACEs are common in aging.** ~3% of subjects >60 yr show large-scale (>1 Mb) mosaic deletions/gains/CNNLOH in blood by SNP array; ~5–18% of 65–108 yr-olds show ACEs with pathogenic mutations in cancer genes (DNMT3A, TET2, ASXL1 dominate). Frequency in <50 yr-olds is <0.2–1%.
3. **Loss of chromosome Y (LOY) is the most common human post-zygotic mutation.** ~20% of men ≥70 yr carry mosaic LOY in ≥10% of blood cells. LOY is associated with all-cause mortality (median survival 5.5 vs 11 yr in 70–83 yr olds), increased risk of non-haematological cancers, Alzheimer's disease, and smoking exposure (dose-dependent, transient — clears after cessation).
4. **ACEs in skin and other non-blood tissues exist at similar density.** Deep sequencing of macroscopically normal sun-exposed skin shows >100 driver mutations per cm² (NOTCH1 bi-allelic inactivation common), implying ACEs precede and underlie eventual cancer development in many tissues.
5. **Revertant mosaicism and rescue lineages can be life-saving.** Pure 45,X is embryonic lethal in 95–99% of cases; the 1/1000–2500 live-birth Turner syndrome cases are essentially all chromosomal mosaics with a 46,XX or 46,XY rescue lineage. Confined placental mosaicism (CPM, ~1–2% of pregnancies) is partly a revertant-mosaicism phenomenon (meiotic CPM = trisomy rescue in embryonic lineage).
6. **Why ACEs accumulate with age** — three coexisting hypotheses: (a) time/cell-divisions accumulate, (b) loss of HSC clonal diversity in elderly makes any single mutated lineage easier to detect (cf. the 115-yr-old whose blood derived from just 2 HSC clones), (c) reduced genomic surveillance with age.

## Methods / evidence

Review of ~120 sources spanning cytogenetics (1960s LOY studies), 2010s SNP-array studies (Jacobs / Laurie / Forsberg LOY cohorts), exome-sequencing CHIP cohorts (Jaiswal, Genovese, McKerrell 2014), single-cell studies (McConnell 2013 neurons, Lodato 2015, Abyzov 2012 iPS-derived CNVs in fibroblasts), and case literature on revertant mosaicism. Three multi-organ surveys (Ref 37–39) compared 3–12 organs per individual by SNP array — small samples but established inter-organ mosaicism as ubiquitous.

## Surprising or load-bearing bits

- **The CNV/SV emphasis was *correct* but lost the framing war.** Post-2018, the CHIP/SNV literature exploded (Jaiswal NEJM 2014/17, Bick 2020), and the structural-variant framing became a niche subfield even as LOY remained the most prevalent single ACE class. This paper is the canonical reference for the "structural variants matter more than SNVs at the cell-burden level" position.
- **LOY-Alzheimer link.** Predates the more recent Y-chromosome-specific work; the 2017 review already cites strong evidence that LOY in blood correlates with AD diagnosis and male/female AD-incidence disparity. Worth following up on whether this hypothesis held up.
- **Hepatocyte polyploidy as physiological mosaicism.** Adult human liver contains a substantial fraction of polyploid hepatocytes (4n, 8n), preferentially pericentral, increasing with age. Discussed as physiological — possibly buffering DNA damage. Connects to the wiki's chromatin-mechanical / structural-variant axes.
- **Microchimerism.** 15–>50% of mothers carry fetal cells in their blood persisting for decades — discussed as a parallel form of "non-self genetic content in the soma" alongside post-zygotic mosaicism.

## Open questions

- The 2017 review predates duplex sequencing's maturation. Many of its bulk-DNA-based ACE-frequency estimates underestimate true mosaicism — single-cell + duplex methods (NanoSeq, META-CS, Duplex-Multiome) revise these upward.
- LOY-Alzheimer's link mechanism remains speculative (immunosurveillance defect? biomarker of aging?). What single-cell evidence has accumulated since?
