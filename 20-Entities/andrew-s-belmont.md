# Andrew S. Belmont

> Professor at University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign; leader in microscopy-based studies of interphase chromosome architecture and gene-tethering experiments. Developed the *lac* operator / *lacI*-GFP system for live-cell tracking of chromosome regions and ectopic-targeting assays that test sequence elements for NL-targeting activity.

## Profile

- Affiliation: Department of Cell and Developmental Biology, UIUC
- Research focus: live-cell chromosome architecture, gene positioning, NL targeting elements, tethering experiments
- Methodological signature: ectopic insertion of LAD-derived sequences + microscopy readout (e.g., Bian 2013, Harr 2015)

## Mentions

- **2026-05-19** — [[10-Summaries/van-steensel-2017-lads-review]]: co-author on the canonical LADs review. Brings the microscopy/tethering perspective complementing van Steensel's DamID perspective.

## Positions and claims

- LAD–NL interactions are *multivalent* — multiple non-overlapping 4–32 kb fragments within a LAD can independently confer NL targeting ([[10-Summaries/van-steensel-2017-lads-review]]).
- Tethering of a viral transcriptional activator to a transgene array at the NL causes long-range, directed *movement* to the nuclear interior — implying an active force-generating mechanism (actin / nuclear myosin 1c implicated) ([[10-Summaries/van-steensel-2017-lads-review]]).
