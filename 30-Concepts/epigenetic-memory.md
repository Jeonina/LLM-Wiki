# Epigenetic memory

> The mitotically heritable record of past cellular states encoded in DNA methylation, histone modifications, and chromatin organization. Cells "remember" their developmental history, tissue of origin, and exposure history even after dedifferentiation or reprogramming.

## Definition

Memory is enforced by **maintenance machinery**: DNMT1+UHRF1 at the replication fork, polycomb (H3K27me3) and trithorax (H3K4me3) systems, heterochromatin self-templating. Stable but not absolute: extended reprogramming or cancer-associated remodeling can erase memory partially.

## Why it matters

- **iPSCs retain methylation signatures** of donor cells and preferentially differentiate back toward original lineage ([[10-Summaries/dna-methylation-an-epigenetic-mark-of-cellular-memory-experimental-molecular-medicine]]).
- **Cancer cells retain methylation** of their tissue of origin even at metastatic sites — enables [[30-Concepts/cancer-of-unknown-primary]] tissue classification by methylation pattern (EPICUP).
- **MSCs** show donor-source-specific methylation persisting through culture, a quality-control biomarker.
