# Lodato et al. 2017 — somatic mutations accumulate in aging neurons

> Thesis: postmitotic neurons live for decades without DNA replication, but they nevertheless accumulate somatic mutations across the human lifespan, and the rate increases under conditions of impaired DNA repair (Cockayne syndrome, xeroderma pigmentosum) and neurodegeneration. Single-neuron MDA-based whole-genome sequencing across subjects from 4 months to 82 years of age provides the first direct measurement of this aging clock at single-cell resolution.

## Key claims (abstract + intro)

- **Neurons accumulate somatic SNVs with age**, despite being postmitotic. Rate measurable across human lifespan via single-neuron MDA + WGS.
- **DNA-repair defects accelerate accumulation**: subjects with Cockayne syndrome or xeroderma pigmentosum show faster somatic mutation rates than controls.
- **Neurodegeneration is associated with elevated somatic mutation load** — direct evidence linking somatic mosaicism to disease.
- Postmitotic mutations might only affect single neurons, but the **accumulated genomic divergence across the brain could affect function**.

## Why this matters

Foundational paper for the **somatic mosaicism in the brain** branch of the wiki. Anchors the rate-of-accumulation claim with quantitative single-neuron data, and links it to disease (Cockayne, XP, neurodegeneration). Frequently co-cited with Bae et al. 2017/2018 (developmental brain mosaicism) and Miller et al. 2022 (Alzheimer's).
