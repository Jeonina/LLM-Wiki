Foundational scWGS study of human cortical neurons. Lodato and colleagues isolated NeuN+ nuclei from postmortem prefrontal cortex of three neurotypical individuals (ages 15, 17, 42), amplified by MDA, and sequenced 36 single neurons at ~40× coverage. Triple-caller intersection identified an average of ~1,685–1,793 somatic SNVs per neuron, dominated by C>T transitions, with significant enrichment in transcribed coding exons of brain-expressed genes and a strand bias consistent with transcription-associated DNA damage rather than replication errors. Single-neuron SNV rates were uniform within each individual but were elevated relative to other postmitotic tissues, suggesting that postmitotic neurons accumulate damage-driven mutations over their lifespan.

The study demonstrated that somatic SNVs serve as durable lineage markers. Nested phylogenies built from shared and private mutations divided the cortex of one brain into at least five clades tracing back to pregastrulation founder cells, with cortical neurons sharing a more recent common ancestor with cardiomyocytes than with cells from other germ layers. Several neurons carried protein-altering mutations in genes implicated in epilepsy (\textit{SCN1A}) and schizophrenia (\textit{SLC12A2}), establishing the principle that even neurotypical brains contain individual neurons with disruptive mutations.

## Why this matters

The first systematic single-neuron WGS demonstration. Anchors §3.1 (genotype-centric scDNA), §5 (neuroscience applications), and the lineage-tracing rationale in §1.
