# CpG island

> Short (typically 200–2000 bp) regions of unusually high CpG dinucleotide density and GC content that occur at the promoters of most housekeeping and developmental regulator genes. Constitutively *unmethylated*, in contrast to the global genome's ~60–80% CpG methylation. Hypermethylation of promoter CpG islands is a hallmark of tumorigenesis ([[10-Summaries/smith-2013-methylation-development]]).

## Definition

Mammalian genomes are globally CpG-depleted (~21% of expected CpG counts), an artifact of the elevated mutation rate of methylated CpG → TpG. CpG islands escape this depletion because they are unmethylated and therefore not exposed to deamination-driven mutation. Functional definition: GC content >50%, observed/expected CpG ratio >0.6, length >200 bp.

~60% of human genes have a CpG island at their promoter; **"orphan" CpG islands** outside promoters also occur and may serve enhancer-like functions.

## Why it matters

- Unmethylated CpG islands at promoters are markers of active or poised transcription.
- Hypermethylation of CpG islands is a major cancer-epigenetics phenomenon — silences tumor suppressors and is a clinical biomarker.
- The maintenance of CpG islands as unmethylated requires *active* exclusion of DNMTs by TF binding, H3K4 methyltransferases, and possibly TET-mediated demethylation as proofreading ([[10-Summaries/smith-2013-methylation-development]]).

## Variants and refinements

- **Promoter CpG islands** — most common; protected from methylation.
- **Orphan CpG islands** — intragenic or distal; enhancer-like, more often regulated.
- **Methylation-sensitive vs methylation-insensitive promoters** — only ~50% of TSS-proximal CpG islands are silenced when methylated.

## Contested points

- Whether orphan CpG islands have distinct regulatory mechanisms or are simply unannotated TSSs.

## Examples

- Hypermethylation of CDKN2A CpG island in glioblastoma → loss of tumor suppressor function.
- Hypermethylation of MLH1 CpG island in colorectal cancer → microsatellite instability.
