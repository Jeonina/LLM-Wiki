# Post-zygotic variation

> Genetic changes acquired after fertilization, not present in the zygote and not transmitted constitutionally through the germline. The unifying term used by Forsberg 2017 to encompass all somatic and germline mosaic mutations ([[10-Summaries/forsberg-2017-mosaicism-review|Forsberg 2017]]).

> [!abstract] 한눈에
> Post-zygotic variation은 수정 이후 한 개인의 일부 세포에서 생긴 DNA 변화로, 체세포·생식세포 mosaic 변이를 모두 묶는 용어다 ([[10-Summaries/forsberg-2017-mosaicism-review|Forsberg 2017]]). 언제 어느 계통에서 생겼는지가 자손 전달 여부와 bulk 조직에서 보이는지를 결정하며, neuron은 태어난 지 1년 안에 이미 수백 개의 sSNV를 지닌다 ([[10-Summaries/lodato-2017-aging-neurons|Lodato 2018]]).

## Definition

Any DNA change — SNV, indel, CNV, structural variant, aneuploidy — that arises in a cell of an individual after fertilization. The mutation may be present in some subset of cells (somatic mosaicism) or in some subset of germ cells (gonadal mosaicism), or both (gonosomal mosaicism) ([[10-Summaries/forsberg-2017-mosaicism-review|Forsberg 2017]]).

Distinguishes from:

- **Constitutional variants** — present in every cell of the individual (germline-transmitted).
- **De novo germline variants** — present in every cell because they arose in a parental gamete or the zygote.
- **Chimerism** — multiple genotypes from multiple fertilization events.
- **[[microchimerism]]** — small populations of cells from a different individual.

## Why it matters

Post-zygotic variation is the molecular substrate of [[40-Topics/somatic-mosaicism]] and the focus of single-cell DNA sequencing for human-development and disease applications. The framing is useful clinically because it predicts:

- Whether a mutation may be transmitted to offspring (depends on whether it predates PGC differentiation — [[developmental-mutation-timing]]).
- Whether bulk DNA from one tissue captures the mutation (depends on which lineages inherit it).

## Variant classes

- **Single-base** (SNV) vs **structural** (indel, CNV, SV) — different detection requirements.
- **Mosaic VAF** — variant allele frequency depends on when and where the mutation arose; ranges from <1% (late, single lineage) to ~25% (first mitosis; ~50% of cells carry the heterozygous variant).

## Evidence and examples

- Mosaic CALR or JAK2 mutations in MPN clonal hematopoiesis ([[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha|Izzo 2024]]).
- CHILD syndrome — midline-demarcated (half-body) phenotype illustrating mutation timing relative to left–right axis determination ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
- Neurons already carry ~300–900 sSNVs within a year of birth, consistent with estimates for fetal neural progenitors ([[10-Summaries/lodato-2017-aging-neurons|Lodato 2018]]; [[10-Summaries/bae-2017-pregastrulation-mutations|Bae 2018]]).

## Contested points

- Whether the term should include normal-aging mutational background (every cell has them) or be restricted to clinically meaningful variants — definitional, not factual.
