# Morriss et al. 2024 — spatial genomics for clonal heterogeneity

> Thesis: clonal heterogeneity in tumors and tissues has been studied either through sequencing (loses spatial information) or through imaging (loses genome-wide variant information). Morriss et al. develop a **spatial genomics method** that captures both clonal genetic information (CNVs, somatic variants) and spatial location simultaneously, enabling multi-modal study of clonal architecture in intact tissues.

## Key claims (abstract + intro)

- **Spatial + genomic readout**: spatial coordinates retained while genome-wide DNA information captured per spot/cell.
- **Clonal heterogeneity mapping**: CNVs, possibly SNVs, can be assigned to spatially-resolved cell populations.
- Bridges to **In Situ Genome Sequencing** (IGS, Payne 2021) and other spatial-DNA modalities, but with greater emphasis on clonal architecture readout.
- Demonstrated in tumor / tissue contexts — clonal subpopulations spatially mapped.

## Why this matters

Establishes **spatial DNA genomics** as a complement to spatial transcriptomics. Critically important for understanding tumor microenvironment, clonal selection in space, and tissue-mosaicism patterns that are invisible to dissociated-cell sequencing. Bridges Morriss/LaFave/Lareau/Macosko labs.
