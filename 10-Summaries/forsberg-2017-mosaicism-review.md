**Citation:** Forsberg et al. (2017) — *Mosaicism in health and disease: clones picking up speed* — *Nature Reviews Genetics*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrg.2016.145)

Forsberg, Gisselsson and Dumanski (Uppsala + Lund) reviewed **post-zygotic variation** as a unifying concept covering all DNA changes acquired after the zygote stage. Argues that emerging studies show post-zygotic variation is far more common than appreciated — no two somatic cells likely share an exact genome. Sections: (i) historical context of focus on germline transmission vs. recent attention to somatic variation; (ii) mosaic loss of chromosome Y (mLOY) and X (mLOX) as common age-related events with disease associations; (iii) microchimerism (maternal, fetal, dizygotic twin-twin, iatrogenic) as a related complicating phenomenon; (iv) implications for clinical genetic testing — mosaic variants are common confounders. Frames CH and mLOY as physiological mosaicism with disease consequences.

## Why this matters

Strong §1/§2/§5 review citation when discussing the prevalence and clinical impact of somatic mosaicism. Complements Campbell 2015 (developmental focus) and Lynch 2010 (mutation rate). Important for argumentation: somatic variation is ubiquitous, so bulk sequencing underestimates the true mutational burden. Existing bibkey `forsberg2017` already present. Particularly useful for §5 aging/CH applications — mLOY discussion is field-defining.
