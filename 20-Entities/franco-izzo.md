# Franco Izzo

> Single-cell genomics methods developer; first author of the GoT–ChA paper (2024). Spent his postdoctoral years in the Landau lab developing the GoT family of methods; now PI at Mount Sinai.

## Profile

Co-author on the original [[30-Concepts/got]] paper ([[10-Summaries/nam-2019-got]], 2019) and first author / corresponding author of the [[30-Concepts/got-cha]] paper ([[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]], 2024). Affiliations: New York Genome Center, Weill Cornell Medicine; present address Department of Oncological Sciences, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai. Email franco.izzo@mssm.edu.

## Mentions

- **2026-05-07** — [[10-Summaries/nam-2019-got]]: co-author on the original GoT paper.
- **2026-05-07** — [[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]]: first/co-corresponding author. Led development of GoT–ChA, which extends GoT to chromatin accessibility and switches the genotype capture from cDNA to gDNA — eliminating the expression-level and transcript-distance limitations that motivated [[30-Concepts/circularization-got]] in the 2019 paper.

## Positions and claims

- Holds that **gDNA-based genotype capture** is the right architectural choice for joint genotype + epigenome single-cell assays, because it decouples genotyping efficiency from gene expression and locus position ([[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]]).
- Holds that **JAK2V617F induces a cell-intrinsic pro-inflammatory chromatin state in HSCs before overt MPN**, reframing inflammation in MPN from microenvironmental to intrinsic ([[10-Summaries/izzo-2024-got-cha]]).
