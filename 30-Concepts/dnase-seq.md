# DNase-seq

> Original genome-scale chromatin accessibility assay using DNase I endonuclease cleavage of accessible (nucleosome-free) DNA. Established the chromatin-accessibility paradigm and produced ENCODE's DNase I hypersensitive site (DHS) atlas, but largely superseded by [[atac-seq]] due to high input requirements (millions of cells).

## Definition

DNase I preferentially cleaves DNA at nucleosome-free, TF-accessible regions. Two original protocols ([[10-Summaries/sandy-2019-naturereviewsgenetics]]):

- **Boyle et al. 2008 (single cut)** — restriction-enzyme-mediated isolation and barcoding of each DNase cut site.
- **Hesselberth et al. 2009 (double cut)** — size selection for fragments arising from paired cleavage events within DHSs.

## Why it matters

- Defined the field of chromatin accessibility prior to ATAC-seq.
- The ENCODE DHS atlas (>100 tissues, cell lines) was generated by DNase-seq and remains a benchmark for accessibility comparisons.
- Sequence-bias profile differs from Tn5 (ATAC-seq) and can be useful for footprinting in some contexts.

## Variants and refinements

- Largely supplanted by [[atac-seq]] for new experiments due to the input-requirement gap (millions of cells vs ~500).

## Contested points

- DNase-seq vs ATAC-seq peak calls show small but real differences at fine scale — most consequential for footprinting.

## Examples

- ENCODE Tier 1 cell line DHS profiles — established the 2–3% / 90% accessibility-vs-TF-binding observation referenced throughout [[10-Summaries/sandy-2019-naturereviewsgenetics]].
