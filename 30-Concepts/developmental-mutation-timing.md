# Developmental mutation timing

> The when of a post-zygotic mutation determines the where: mutations early in embryogenesis affect a large fraction of the soma; mutations later are progressively restricted to specific tissue lineages or to a single side of the body. The framework that connects mutation timing to clinical phenotype and to transmission risk ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).

## Definition

The geometry of mutation distribution as a function of embryonic timing, a form of [[30-Concepts/post-zygotic-variation|post-zygotic variation]] ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]] Fig 1).

## Timing windows

| Mutation arises | Cells or tissues affected | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| First mitosis | ~50% of all cells | Possibly identical twins discordant for a new dominant mutation | ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review\|Campbell 2015]]) |
| Before left–right determination | Both sides of the body; potentially one or both gonads | — | ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review\|Campbell 2015]]) |
| After left–right determination | One side only; at most one gonad | — | ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review\|Campbell 2015]]) |
| After primordial germ cell (PGC) differentiation (~24 days post-fertilization, after ~15 mitoses) | Somatic *or* germline lineages, never both | — | ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review\|Campbell 2015]]) |

## Why it matters

The framework inverts the diagnostic question: from the phenotype distribution of a mosaic disease, the **clinician can infer** the approximate timing of the causative mutation ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]), which in turn predicts:

- Recurrence risk to siblings, which depends on whether the mutation predates PGC differentiation ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
- Tissues likely to carry the mutation (informs choice of biopsy site).
- Severity (early mutations affect more cells, often more severe).

## Evidence and examples

- **PGC bottleneck timing:** ~15 mitotic divisions, day 24 post-fertilization in humans ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
- **Early genome instability:** up to 70% of human embryos show ≥1 aneuploid blastomere in week 1; early development is genome-unstable, but most aneuploid cells are selected against ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
- **CHILD syndrome:** its midline demarcation illustrates mutation timing relative to left–right determination ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
- **Pallister-Killian syndrome:** isochromosome 12p, lethal as constitutional, viable as mosaic ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
- **Linear and whorled nevoid hypermelanosis:** Blashko lines reflect post-determination clonal migration patterns.

## Contested points

- The exact timing of left–right determination in humans is unknown (mouse: ~8-cell stage) ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
- Whether the elevated mutation rate in early embryos reflects intrinsic mutability (maternal-cytoplasm replication machinery) or relaxed selection ([[10-Summaries/campbell-2015-mosaicism-review|Campbell 2015]]).
