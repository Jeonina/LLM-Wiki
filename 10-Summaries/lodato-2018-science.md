Lodato et al. sequenced 161 neurons from prefrontal cortex (PFC) and dentate gyrus (DG) of 15 neurotypical individuals (ages 4 months to 82 years) plus 42 neurons from 9 patients with early-onset DNA-repair-deficient neurodegenerative syndromes (Cockayne syndrome, xeroderma pigmentosum). Somatic SNVs were called by a new linked-read analysis pipeline (LiRA) that uses germline-SNP phasing to distinguish true single-stranded variants from MDA artifacts.

Three key findings. (1) Single-neuron sSNV counts increase approximately linearly with age in both PFC (~16–21 sSNVs/year) and DG (~40 sSNVs/year, ~2-fold higher than PFC). The authors named this age-related accumulation "genosenium." (2) Cockayne syndrome and xeroderma pigmentosum neurons show ~2.3-fold and ~2.5-fold excess sSNVs over age-matched controls, demonstrating that defective nucleotide excision repair accelerates neuronal mutation accumulation. (3) Three NMF-derived mutational signatures dissect the mutagenic forces: Signature A (clock-like, C>T, accumulates with age, region-independent), Signature B (DG-enriched, C>T, oxidative-damage-like), and Signature C (enriched in CS/XP cases, T>C, repair-deficiency-associated). Signature A closely resembles COSMIC SBS5/1 and is shared with dividing somatic tissues; Signature B suggests a region-specific neurogenic-niche source.

## Why this matters

The canonical demonstration that postmitotic human neurons accumulate damage-driven mutations linearly with age, and that DNA-repair defects accelerate this process. Anchors §3.1 (genotype-centric), §5 (aging and neurodegeneration applications), §6 (mutational-signature framework).
