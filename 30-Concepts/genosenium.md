# Genosenium

> The accumulated somatic-mutation landscape of aged cells and tissues — the "aged genome" considered as a phenotype. Frames somatic mosaicism as a hallmark of aging analogous to telomere shortening or epigenetic drift (synthesis; usage observed in [[10-Summaries/vijg-2020-cell]]; [[10-Summaries/cagan-2022-nature]]).

## Definition

Coined informally in the somatic-mutation-and-aging literature, "genosenium" denotes the cumulative mutation burden that distinguishes aged from young cells, together with the cell-type-specific selection pressures that shape which clones expand. Distinct from senescence (a cellular phenotype) — focuses on the genomic substrate.

## Evidence

- Somatic mutation rate scales with lifespan across mammals ([[10-Summaries/cagan-2022-nature]]).
- Aged human cells accumulate ~50–200 SNVs/year tissue-dependent ([[10-Summaries/vijg-2020-cell]]; [[10-Summaries/lodato-2017-aging-neurons]]).
- Cell-type-specific clonal expansion in aged hematopoiesis ([[10-Summaries/kapadia-2024-stem-cell-aging]]).
- ~6% of aged brains carry >100 detectable somatic SNVs, possibly precursor states for glioma ([[10-Summaries/taejeong-2022-science]]).

## Open question

Is genosenium *causal* for aging-related disease, or a *biomarker*? Distinction has therapeutic implications.
